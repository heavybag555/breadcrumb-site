# Design Leadership Thesis — 12-Minute Presentation Plan

Three artifacts, one thesis, at three distances:

- **Activation Plan (Personal Operating System)** — the private operating system: how I run the studio
- **Essay (*IE Blacktops, LA Terminals*)** — the public articulation: why this moment rewards the translator designer
- **Website (`2u4u.studio`)** — the living expression: the deployment link that proves both

Walk it inside-out: plan → essay → site.

---

## Cold open — upbringing hook (~40 sec, before Part 1)

Deliver this slowly. No slide, or a single black slide with the title. Let the room settle before you pivot into the studio.

> "Hemet, California. I'm ten years old, alone in an elementary school computer lab, half an hour after the last bell. Both of my parents are teachers, so the school day never really ended for me — it just turned into time. Six years of after-school hours on a keyboard turned into something I didn't have a name for yet: a voice, a way of making, and a belief I got from watching them — that knowledge is most meaningful when it's given away. Fifteen years later, that belief has a studio behind it. The next 12 minutes are how I'm running it — the plan, the essay, and the site."

**Alternate (shorter, more scene-forward):**

> "I grew up in Hemet, California, the kid of two teachers. The last bell of the day was for everyone but me. While the other kids went home, I stayed on the blacktop drawing imaginary soccer jerseys in chalk, or snuck back into the computer lab. Fifteen years later, I can finally make sense of the habit. Everything I'm about to show you came out of those hours."

## Part 1 — Activation Plan / Personal Operating System (4:00)

**Purpose:** show a designed trajectory with operational rigor. This is a career compass + leadership strategy + 90-day plan, not a wish list.

| Beat | Time | What you say / show |
|---|---|---|
| North Star, read verbatim | 0:00–0:30 | One slide, one sentence: *"I want to build 2u4u.studio into a respected independent design practice that competes on quality, not volume — the kind of studio that smaller brands and emerging cultural projects turn to when they want work that is rigorous and considered."* Name the reference points: **Mouthwash Studio · Porto Rocha.** Say what success isn't ("not headcount, not revenue targets") and what it is ("a body of published work, clients who return, peer recognition"). |
| The studio, by the numbers | 0:30–1:15 | One slide, tight fact sheet:<br>• **1 person · 1 roof · 1 voice**<br>• **5 services:** Development · Design · Prototyping · Art Direction · Photo<br>• **6 client types:** independent fashion/apparel · tech · architecture + built environment · cultural organizations · product startups · other studios needing overflow<br>• **5 projects shipped** since fall 2025<br>Frame: *"Vertically integrated — the same person who codes the site directs the photo shoot and builds the identity system."* |
| Differentiation + how clients arrive | 1:15–2:15 | Why one-person is a feature, not a constraint: **no translation loss, no handoffs, no account manager.** Then the three-channel acquisition model:<br>**1. Warm referral** — every project produces one introduction<br>**2. Published case studies** — five projects documented with process, decisions, outcomes<br>**3. Direct cold outreach** — ten specific, non-generic notes to brands with weak web presence; expect one or two<br>Business thesis in one sentence: *"Small brands pay for coordination overhead when they hire specialists. This studio collapses that overhead."* |
| Leadership strategy — the three that matter most | 2:15–3:15 | Pick the three that carry the POV best:<br>• **Pitching** → "You're hiring a single person who can hold the whole thing in their head."<br>• **Business value** → outcomes over outputs: before/after on the problem, client-reported conversion, complexity reduced.<br>• **Personal discipline** → protect the morning for deep work · separate making from managing · define a good week concretely (2 hrs deep work/day · 1 new outreach · 1 business-side task moved).<br>Optional (+10 sec): *"If a client relationship becomes extractive, the corrective is simple — do not renew. Build the runway to say no."* |
| 90-day plan — the operating cadence | 3:15–3:45 | One slide, three columns. Read the top bullet of each, not the whole list:<br>• **Month 1 — Positioning:** document the five projects as case studies; publish the thesis essay<br>• **Month 2 — Activation:** 10 cold outreach notes · referral ask to every existing client · 5–8 ArtCenter alumni reach-outs<br>• **Month 3 — Conversion:** close or formally propose to one new client · establish one retainer · revise the North Star based on what three months taught me |
| Bridge to essay | 3:45–4:00 | "A plan that only lives in a Notion doc isn't an operating system — it's a diary. The next layer is putting the POV on record, publicly." |

**Tip:** have the Notion page open in a tab scrolled to Part 1D so you can click through to the 90-day plan if anyone pushes on it in Q&A. The plan is the strongest credibility signal — it's the part most peers won't have.

## Part 2 — The Essay: *IE Blacktops, LA Terminals* (4:00)

**Purpose:** show that a POV can be turned into a public, cite-able argument. The essay is the leadership test — not just a builder, but someone willing to *name the shift*.

| Beat | Time | What you say / show |
|---|---|---|
| Why this title | 0:00–0:20 | *"IE = Inland Empire blacktops; LA = the terminals I now live in, both literal and CLI. Geography became a stack."* Put the Medium hero image on screen. |
| Origin → present (30 sec, not more) | 0:20–0:50 | Parents as teachers → after-school computer labs → 15 years later: designer + educator sharing one timeline at Fuser + ArtCenter. Don't retell the childhood; the site's Info page does that. |
| The study: Ryo Lu + Anthropic | 0:50–2:00 | Two logos on one slide. Ryo: *"code is clay… you learn by making, not planning."* Anthropic: design lives at the model level, not just the interface. The through-line you found: **the most impactful designers right now are fluent in systems — a codebase, an org, or a model's values.** |
| The forecast | 2:00–3:00 | Quote Jenny Wen: *"the design process we've treated as gospel is basically dead."* Quote Dylan Field: AI inverted the design↔product dynamic — **craft is now the competitive advantage.** Land the "translator designer" — taste + implementation — as the role this moment rewards. |
| Your POV (the risk) | 3:00–3:40 | The opinion you're willing to be wrong about on record: *"I find it difficult to fully trust software leadership that lacks design education."* And the reframe: agents don't kill systems design, they **raise the premium on it**. |
| Bridge to site | 3:40–4:00 | "I could have just written this. Instead I built the studio the essay argues for." Cut directly to the live URL. |

**Tip:** keep the Medium article open in a second tab, scrolled to the must-haves section. If anyone challenges the argument in Q&A, you're one click from the receipts.

### The four must-haves (keep visible during Beat 3)

1. Abandon ownership for impact — there's no time for gatekeeping
2. Move from presenter to participant — get into the codebase
3. Tool mastery is a trap — be tool-agile instead
4. System taste rules

## Transition lines (memorize verbatim)

1. **Plan → Essay:** "A plan that only lives in a Notion doc isn't an operating system — it's a diary."
2. **Essay → Site:** "I could have just written this. Instead I built the studio the essay argues for."
3. **Site → Close:** "The plan tells me how I'll run it. The essay tells the field why. The site is the deployment link — the only deliverable that actually proves either."

## Closing line

> "Mouthwash and Porto Rocha didn't start as ten-person teams. They started as a clear point of view, published early, with work that was worth returning to. That's what this document, this essay, and this site are — the first version of mine."

## Rehearsal checklist

- [ ] Full run-through at actual speaking pace on Day 1 — Parts 2 and 3 will both want to run long; cut ruthlessly so each lands on 4:00.
- [ ] One anchor slide per part visible the whole time (title + timer dot) so you feel the pacing.
- [ ] Preload three tabs *before* you start — Notion POS, Medium essay, live site. Don't navigate during the 12 minutes.
- [ ] Backup: 30-second screen recording of the home scroll in case venue Wi-Fi is bad.
- [ ] Rehearse the three transition lines verbatim — they're the hinges that make it feel like one argument, not three show-and-tells.
